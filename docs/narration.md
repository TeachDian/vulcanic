# Recording the narration

The app reads each component aloud. Out of the box it uses the phone's speech engine with a male voice. To use a real voice instead, record the five passages below and add them to the app. Any passage without a recording keeps the phone voice, so you can add them one at a time.

## How

1. Record each passage as its own file. A phone voice memo in a quiet room is enough. Leave half a second of silence at each end.
2. Save it as `.m4a` (or `.mp3`, `.ogg`, `.wav`, `.aac`) with the file name shown above its passage.
3. Put the files in `assets/narration/`.
4. Rebuild the app. Nothing else needs changing.

The words do not have to match exactly: the recording is played as it is.

## Script

### 01 Volcanic cone

File: `assets/narration/cone.m4a`

> Volcanic cone. The mountain an eruption builds. The cone is the volcano you see from the outside. It grows layer by layer from the lava, ash and rock thrown out by earlier eruptions. This one is a stratovolcano: steep near the summit, with a bowl shaped crater at the top where the vent opens.

### 02 Rock layers

File: `assets/narration/terrain.m4a`

> Rock layers. The crust beneath the volcano. Under the volcano lies the crust: bands of older rock laid down over millions of years. Magma forces its way up through cracks in these layers. The cutaway shows them from the side, like the layers in a slice of cake.

### 03 Magma chamber

File: `assets/narration/chamber.m4a`

> Magma chamber. Where molten rock waits. The magma chamber is a reservoir of molten rock stored deep underground. As more magma arrives and the gas dissolved in it builds up, the pressure rises until the rock above can no longer hold it, and the magma is forced upward.

### 04 Main conduit

File: `assets/narration/conduit.m4a`

> Main conduit. The passage to the surface. The conduit is the pipe that connects the magma chamber to the crater. During an eruption, magma races up it as the gas inside expands into bubbles, the same way a shaken bottle of soda fizzes over when it is opened.

### 05 Lava flow

File: `assets/narration/lava.m4a`

> Lava flow. Molten rock above ground. Once magma reaches the surface it is called lava. It spills from the crater and runs downhill, glowing orange while it is hot. As it cools it darkens and hardens into solid rock, adding a new layer to the cone.
